In a sunlit garden workshop, a careful caretaker named Mira prepared three plots. She knew seeds needed warmth, moisture, and air to sprout. For the first plot, she kept the soil dry. For the second, she soaked it but sealed it in a jar with no air. For the third, she gave it water, warmth, and loose soil. The fourth plot she left cold and damp.

Days passed. Only the third plot showed green shoots. The others remained bare. Mira’s neighbor, a gardener who watered every plot equally, scoffed. “Your method is too fussy,” he said. Mira replied, “Let’s test it.” She took seeds from the dry plot and placed them in warm, moist, airy soil. They sprouted within a week. She took seeds from the sealed jar and planted them in open, warm soil—they also grew. But seeds from the cold plot, even with water and air, stayed dormant until moved to warmth.

Mira gathered her apprentices. “The seeds need all three conditions together,” she said. “Missing one stops the whole process.” They then checked each plot’s conditions daily, confirming that growth only followed when warmth, water, and air were present.
