In the garden workshop, a careful caretaker named Mira prepared a sunlit plot, ensuring the soil was rich and the leaves of her young plants were deep green with chlorophyll. She knew that without light, no true growth could occur.

Each morning, the plants stretched toward the sun, using its energy to transform water and air into starch, their stems thickening and leaves unfurling. Nearby, a compost pile breathed out warmth, a reminder that all living things consume and release. Weeds competed for the light, threatening to overshadow Mira’s crop.

To test if the growth was genuine, Mira covered one plant with a dark cloth for three days. Its leaves paled and drooped, producing no new starch. When she uncovered it, the plant slowly greened again, proving that light was the hidden prerequisite.

Later, as an exercise, Mira placed a jar of pondweed in water at different distances from a lamp. At 10 cm, bubbles streamed out; at 40 cm, they barely appeared. In darkness, none came. She understood: the process of capturing light energy was the only source of this visible life.
